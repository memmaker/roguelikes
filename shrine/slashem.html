Lineage

SLASH'EM is a NetHack variant built from two older NetHack mods. Tom Proudfoot's SLASH (February 1996, from his own NetHack-- and parts of Stephen White's NetHack Plus) was synchronised with NetHack 3.2 by Enrico Horn as SLASH 4.1.2. Larry Stewart-Zerba's Wizard Patch (April 1996 to April 1997, later with Warwick Allison) enhanced the Wizard's spellcasting. Warren Cheung combined SLASH 4.1.2 and the Wizard Patch into SLASH'EM 0.1 in November 1997.

The code then followed NetHack upstream: 0.0.6 was merged onto NetHack 3.3, and 0.0.7 (the version here, 0.0.7E7F3) onto NetHack 3.4.3. Sources: dat/history and the Guidebook's history chapter. See the family tree, next to NetHack 5.0.

Trivia

  • The name stands for “Super Lotsa Added Stuff Hack – Extended Magic”. (NetHackWiki)
  • The dates disagree: the game's own history file says SLASH'EM 0.1 appeared in November 1997, NetHackWiki says Warren Cheung released it on 7 January 1998. (NetHackWiki)
  • 0.0.7E7F3 (30 December 2006) is the last stable release; an alpha 0.0.8E0F1 came out the same day. (slashem.sourceforge.net)
  • It is considered much more difficult than vanilla NetHack. (NetHackWiki)
  • Sokoban is among the features NetHack adopted from SLASH'EM. (RogueBasin)
  • Its vampire race and Black Market level were taken over by other variants such as UnNetHack; Slash'EM Extended and SlashTHEM continue it. (NetHackWiki)
  • According to NetHackWiki, as of March 2026 no fork of SLASH'EM is maintained. (NetHackWiki)
  • 3D and isometric front-ends exist: noegnud and Vulture. (Wikipedia)

What's unique

  • 18 roles, 10 races. New roles Flame Mage, Ice Mage, Necromancer, Undead Slayer and Yeoman; new races doppelganger, drow, hobbit, lycanthrope and vampire, next to NetHack's 13 roles and 5 races.
  • Techniques (#technique, T): 41 role and race powers with a cooldown, from the Samurai's kiii and the Healer's surgery to the Necromancer's raise zombies and the Monk's spirit bomb.
  • A much bigger dungeon. Grund's Stronghold, the Wyrm Caves, One-eyed Sam's Black Market, the Lost Tomb, the Spider Caves, the Sunless Sea, the Temple of Moloch, the Giant Caverns and Frankenstein's Lab; special levels such as the rat and kobold levels, the storerooms, the nymph level, a Mall and the Guild of Disgruntled Adventurers.
  • Alignment quests: besides the role Quest, a Lawful, Neutral and Chaotic Quest, each a single level behind a portal.
  • Lightsabers and firearms (pistols to rocket launchers), medical kits, orbs, and edible killers: the “killer food ration” is a monster.

Code dive

  • Language: K&R-era C: 184,611 lines in 214 files under src/ and include/; 344,004 lines in 535 .c/.h/.y/.l files counting ports and tools (upstream commit ab6287b).
  • Platforms: sys/ has Unix, MS-DOS, Windows NT and CE, Mac, OS/2, Amiga, Atari, BeOS and VMS. Window ports in win/: tty, X11, Qt, GTK, GNOME, Win32, GEM, an OpenGL port (gl, glHack) and a proxy port for remote interfaces.
  • Data tables: monsters in src/monst.c, objects in src/objects.c, artifacts in include/artilist.h, roles and races in src/role.c, techniques in src/tech.c. Levels are written in .des files compiled by lev_comp (yacc/lex), the dungeon layout in dat/dungeon.def by dgn_comp. Save files are raw structs.
  • USER_SOUNDS: regex rules map messages to sound files; our port uses the same hook for its sound effects.
  • Our port: a web window port (win/web/winweb.c) built with Emscripten, auto-explore, walking to stairs, an Enter menu of all commands, and autosave. The level compilers run under node because wasm32's 4-byte long changes the data file headers.
  • Source: memmaker/slashem; upstream is the SourceForge tarball se007e7f3.tar.gz; our changes.

Manual

The SLASH'EM Guidebook (text, 3,960 lines, December 2003 edition) shipped with the source, under the NetHack General Public License. Its last chapter is the history these pages quote. The in-game ? help and the port's help page cover the keys.

Getting started

  1. Open the game, type a name and answer n to pick role, race, gender and alignment (a Valkyrie or a Samurai forgives mistakes), or y for random.
  2. Move with the arrow keys or hjklyubn; walk into a monster to attack. ~ explores on its own, > walks to the stairs you have seen.
  3. Press Enter for a menu of every command, i for the inventory (also shown in its own window).
  4. Try your role's technique with #technique; pray (#pray) when hit points are low and you have not prayed in a long time.
  5. Eat when “Hungry”, keep your pet close, and expect to die more often than in NetHack: the full key list is in the in-game guide.

Help

There is no walkthrough as such; the closest is NetHackWiki's Standard strategy (SLASH'EM), from character creation to the endgame. Rules of thumb from it:

  • Gain experience level 2 on the first two dungeon levels before going deeper.
  • Press p in a shop for the shopkeeper's services, such as identification and rustproofing.
  • Drain resistance matters more than in NetHack (wands of draining, deep dragons).
  • Don't fight One-eyed Sam head on in the Black Market.
  • More: NetHackWiki: SLASH'EM, RogueBasin, the source's own doc/strategy.txt.

Cheats

  • Wizard mode (slashem -D) is compiled in, but the browser page never passes -D (and the web build can't look up the user it checks), so it can't be reached here.
  • Explore mode works: X or #explore turns the game into a non-scoring discovery game, with no way back.
  • Save scumming works in the browser: Export save before something risky, Import save if it goes wrong.
  • No exploits are listed here: none were checked on a fetched page.
